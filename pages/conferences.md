## CCP5 abstract

Pillared graphene frameworks offer tunable confined space for hydrogen storage, but it remains unclear whether uptake is governed by local graphene-CNT junction chemistry or by the larger framework architecture. Molecular dynamics simulations at 77 K and 10 bar compare pristine graphene, three junction-bearing monolayers, and two pillared bilayer frameworks. The monolayer junction models retain graphene-like gravimetric densities of 5.86-5.92 wt%, showing that CNT fusion can be introduced without sacrificing H<sub>2</sub> uptake. Surface-resolved analysis identifies the CNT base as the stronger-binding local site, while the adjacent graphene edge behaves much like pristine graphene. In bilayer frameworks, raising the pillar count from one to five increases storage from 3.25 to 5.43 wt% by preventing restacking and preserving accessible interlayer volume. These results identify interlayer accessibility as the dominant design lever in 3D graphene-CNT hydrogen-storage frameworks.

**Keywords:** Hydrogen storage, graphene-CNT framework, junction topology, pillar density, physisorption, molecular dynamics.
